How to Write ChatGPT Prompts That Actually Work (With Examples)

The four-part formula behind every ChatGPT prompt that gets a useful answer on the first try — plus before/after examples you can copy.

Most bad ChatGPT answers aren't the model's fault — they're the prompt's fault. Ask a vague question and you get a vague answer; the fix isn't a magic phrase, it's structure.

The four-part formula

Every reliable prompt has four ingredients, in this order: a role, a task, constraints, and a format. Skip any one of them and you're leaving ChatGPT to guess.

  • Role — who should ChatGPT act as? ("Act as a hiring manager...")
  • Task — the specific thing you want done, not the topic in general.
  • Constraints — length, tone, audience, what to avoid.
  • Format — bullet points, a table, a script, a numbered list.

Before and after

Weak prompt: "Write about marketing for my bakery." That's a topic, not an instruction — ChatGPT has to guess your tone, length, and goal.

Strong prompt: "Act as a marketing strategist. Write 5 Instagram captions for a home bakery launching a fall pumpkin bread, casual and warm tone, under 150 characters each." Same topic, four times more useful.

Three mistakes that quietly ruin prompts

  • Asking for everything at once — split multi-step requests into a sequence of prompts instead.
  • Forgetting to say who the output is for — "explain X" to a beginner is a different answer than to an expert.
  • Not giving ChatGPT permission to ask questions back when your request is ambiguous.
